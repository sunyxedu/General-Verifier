Test-time Verification

Design of a meta-harness for a general verifier: sidecar form · single trigger rule · verification-driven search
Design draft v0.4 · July 2026 · project codename General Verifier · (v0.4 addition: §19 Implementation and experiments — all mechanisms have been implemented per this design and have passed the viability experiments; three design corrections forced out by the experiments are in §19.3)

1 Abstract

This report presents the complete design of a general verifier. The core problem it addresses: having an LLM judge "is this answer right?" is unreliable — both because the truth of many propositions lives in the environment rather than in the text, and because the verification process itself is verified by no one. Our approach writes no domain knowledge into prompts; instead, three meta-level mechanisms force correct verification behavior to emerge: shift the audit target from the answer to the verification process itself; replace "judging" with "games" whose outcomes are mechanically observable; and move domain knowledge from prompts to runtime.

Architecturally, the verifier is a standalone sidecar service: the solver is a stock Claude Code, zero modification. The system's only input is an append-only unified event stream; scheduling has exactly one trigger rule — "when an unverified claim gains a dependent, verify it"; the verification method by default goes straight to the strongest reachable one (strength-first), wrong variants constitute a validity exam for the method in use, and downgrade happens only on unreachability; the product is a certificate with a self-contained evidence chain that can be independently rechecked. Finally, a trustworthy verifier makes parallel search possible: the solver uses its built-in subagents to explore multiple hypotheses in parallel and prunes by verification results — test-time compute is thereby safely converted into correctness. The promised scope of this design is the mechanically checkable domain (mathematics, code, executable computation); cost is not a design constraint, and the method policy is strength-first.

v0.3 scope and policy statement: ① The promised scope of this design is the mechanically checkable domain (mathematics, code, executable computation); retrieval/statistics/convention-dependent tasks are extended-domain demonstrations, outside the promised scope. ② Cost is not a design constraint: the method selection policy is "strength-first, downgrade on failure" — by default go straight to the strongest applicable instrument (formalization in the math domain); the two gates degenerate into a validity exam for the strongest method; downgrade happens only on unreachability, never on expense; the original cost-sensitive ordering is demoted to a configurable option. ③ Two load-bearing points that remain LLM judgment are honestly marked (the entailment audit at the demonstrated/corroborated levels), along with the correlation between loops one and three (shared variant generator). ④ A trusted computing base (TCB) statement and an intellectual lineage are newly added.

2 The problem: why "asking the model again" is not verification

The most common verification approach is LLM-as-a-judge: show the answer and the reasoning to another (or the same) model and let it output right/wrong. This practice has two fundamental failure roots; the two exist independently, and neither can be fixed by editing prompts.

Figure 1 · The two independent failure roots of LLM-as-judge: the truth is not in the text; the verification process is itself unverified

Root one (the grounding problem): the truth of many claims can only be determined by interacting with the environment — code must be run, theorems must be formally checked, facts must be traced to primary sources. A pure-reasoning verifier is in principle ineffective on them. Root two (the process-legitimacy problem): even when a verifier claims to have "verified", the way it did so may not be the way we expected; its saying pass does not mean the claim is true, because what we trust is a process that has never been audited. The second root is more hidden and more critical — it means that simply stacking more verifiers does no good, because each new verifier brings a new unaudited process.

3 Three design shifts and one overarching principle

Shift one: the judger's audit target changes from "the answer" to "the verification process". First distinguish three roles: the solver produces answers; the verifier checks answers (the discharge mechanism in this report, components 17–20); the judger checks the quality of the verifier's work — in this architecture it lands as the entailment auditor (21) and the coverage auditor (22), collectively called "the auditors" below. In the naive design, the judger judges the answer once more — that merely introduces yet another untrustworthy verifier: same base model, same material, same blind spots, and it immediately faces the infinite regress of "who judges this judger". The correct way to compress the regress: each upper layer audits only whether the lower layer's obligation set is complete and whether its evidence really discharges the obligations. These two judgments are far smaller and far more mechanical than the original problem (verification asymmetry), and the bottom layer is anchored on machine-checkable artifacts (execution logs, counterexamples, proof files). To be honest: this anchor is fully mechanical only at the proven level; at the demonstrated / corroborated levels, the entailment audit is still a compressed, focused, instrumented LLM judgment — what this report claims is compression and auditability, not elimination (see also §18).

Shift two: replace "judgment" with "mechanism". "Is this verification strict enough", "should we use Lean", "when should we verify" — LLMs are poorly calibrated on such meta-judgments and cannot be trusted with them. The solution is to replace every meta-judgment with a game whose outcome is mechanically observable: the efficacy of a verification method is adjudicated by an exam of wrong variants and its landing point is determined by reachability (the two gates, §7); the timing of verification is determined by the dependency structure (the single trigger rule, §9). Intelligence comes not from exhortations in prompts but from the selection pressure exerted by mechanisms.

Shift three: move domain knowledge from prompts to runtime. All prompt templates are forever generic ("generate falsifiable necessary conditions", "generate plausible-but-wrong variants"). Domain content has three runtime sources: the environment itself (documentation, installed tools, retrievable material), the model's latent knowledge (elicited on the spot by generic meta-instructions), and the system's own failure record (every overturned verdict settles into memory, growing stronger with use).

Overarching principle (used throughout this report): whatever is delegated to the model for generation may affect efficiency, but never correctness. Failures of obligation generation are backstopped by the coverage audit; failures of variant generation are reinforced by the backflow of overturned cases; failures of method ordering are backstopped by the stopping condition; failures of dependency extraction are backstopped by the tamper audit; failures of the orchestration protocol are backstopped by the certificate gate. Behind every act of "generation" stands an independent gate; the gates are graded in mechanicalness — fully mechanical at the proven level, and compressed small-step judgments elsewhere (see the note in §8).

Intellectual lineage

Every component of this design has precedents; the innovation lies in the combination and the mechanization: the certificate is close in form to proof-carrying code (a deliverable, independently recheckable evidence package); the discrimination game is a mechanized generalization of mutation testing and of the negative control from scientific methodology; the taint invariant comes from taint tracking; the cross-branch reuse of refuted conclusions has the shape of CDCL (conflict clause learning) in SAT solvers; the division of labor between the two gates answers the LCF-style kernel discipline (trust converging onto a small kernel); graded verdicts make the hierarchy of evidence strength explicit.

4 System form: standing outside the solver

The form decision of v0.2: the verifier is a sidecar service, and the solver is a stock Claude Code, zero modification. The entire contact surface between the two is three attachment points: hooks (PostToolUse / Stop push every tool call and the session end first-hand to the sidecar), the session log (a structured, append-only complete trace that can be tailed incrementally), and an MCP server (which the solver voluntarily calls for commit / verify / certificate). Any agent with a trace can be verified by the same service — that is exactly what "general" means.

Figure 2 · The stage: the conversational main path ends at Claude Code — it retrieves the certificate via MCP and delivers "answer + certificate conclusion" to the user; only enforcement parties such as CI bypass the solver and fetch the authoritative copy directly

This form brings an enforcement inversion: the system cannot (and no longer tries to) stop the solver from doing anything — coercive power moves to the consumption side. The certificate is a product with a self-contained evidence chain that can be independently rechecked; CI and users treat it as an admission condition ("if the slice contains unverified / refuted, do not merge"). There are two delivery paths: the conversational main path ends at Claude Code — the solver retrieves the certificate via MCP per the protocol and attaches the conclusion level to its final reply; retelling may distort, so the certificate carries a content hash, and any consumer can request the authoritative copy from the sidecar to check against. The enforcement path bypasses the solver — CI obtains the certificate directly from the sidecar. An optional strict mode uses Claude Code's PreToolUse hook to block only "outbound and irreversible" actions (push, publish, deploy): nothing leaves with an unverified slice — the taint rule lands exactly where errors are about to become expensive. The solver's cooperation is entirely voluntary and does not affect soundness: its cooperation only buys precision and savings (see §10); what makes it willing to cooperate is the carrot — a refuted verdict comes with a counterexample, an order of magnitude cheaper than discovering it at CI.

6 Component-by-component

Explained one by one in Figure 3's numbering. Each component does exactly one thing; all coupling goes through the event stream (5) or the claim store (10) — there are no hidden channels.

Adapter layer (1–4): the sole duty is to turn any source into events

1hooks adapter — receives Claude Code's PostToolUse / Stop hook pushes: the name, arguments, and result of every tool call arrive first-hand, and session end produces a stop event. The payload carries an agent identifier, so subagent activity naturally bears branch labels. The optional strict mode uses PreToolUse to intercept outbound irreversible actions.

2log tail adapter — incrementally reads the session trace (structured, append-only). A fallback channel that works even if the solver does not cooperate at all; it also makes any third-party agent with a trace plug-and-verify.

3MCP server — exposes three tools: commit(claim, deps), verify(target), certificate(). The solver's voluntary cooperation channel: cooperation buys precision and feedback speed, not soundness (see §10).

4file replay adapter — replays a historical trace or a plain-text solution into events in order. The so-called "offline mode" is exactly this very-high-latency stream.

Sources of truth (5, 10, 11)

5unified event stream — the system's only input, append-only. tool_call, tool_result, message, commit, stop, verify requests — everything is an event; triggers are events too. It doubles as the event log: resume-from-checkpoint, full audit, replay ablation — three capabilities, one dataset.

10claim store (dependency graph) — a materialized view of the event stream. It stores each claim's content, kind (assertion / effect / final), dependency edges, branch labels, and verification state machine (unverified → verified(grade) / refuted / stale). Claims are keyed by content hash: dedup, caching, and cross-branch reuse come for free.

11artifact store — a content-addressed evidence warehouse: execution logs, counterexamples, proof files, source snapshots. The certificate's evidence chain points here.

Graph construction layer (6–9): everything is a proposal; audits decide truth

6mechanical parser — deterministically converts structured tool events into effect nodes (zero LLM). An effect node is not a special category; it is an ordinary assertion whose content is "this call happened and returned X" — it just happens to have a free, unambiguous registration window (see §9).

7assertion extractor — an LLM role that cuts free text into candidate claims. It carries a cadence knob: the more frequently it extracts, the earlier triggers fire and the earlier losses are cut; the knob affects only feedback latency, never correctness. Prefer coarse granularity over fine: what cannot be split gets merged into a coarse claim.

8dependency-edge proposer — proposes dependency edges for claims. The output is called "proposals" because it is not trusted — it must pass 9.

9tamper auditor — audits all proposals by the variant method, regardless of who proposed them: edges proposed by the extractor are audited, and edges the solver declares itself via MCP are audited the same way (the solver's cooperation is uniformly understood as "volunteer extraction"). Method: corrupt some intermediate conclusion in the trace; the rebuilt graph must contain a path to final, otherwise an edge was missed and extraction is redone.

Trigger layer (12–13)

12trigger — the system's only scheduling rule: when an unverified claim gains its first dependent, verify it. There is no second rule; the eager/lazy distinction, effect special-casing, and Stop special-casing do not exist. It is the constructive version of the taint invariant (final must not depend on unverified). In search scenarios it extends one step without adding rules: adding try-effort to a branch = depending on that branch's frontier claims ("investment is use").

13backward slicer — a certificate request = adding a dependent to final, a special case of the same rule: trace back from final along dependency edges, and verify every residual unverified on the slice. Nodes off the slice (including all dead branches) cost nothing.

Verification kernel (14–23, mode-agnostic, kept verbatim from v0.1)

14obligation compiler — looks only at the task type, never at the candidate's content (answer-blind, to prevent rationalization), and generates the set of necessary conditions that "any correct answer must satisfy". Templates are generic; domain content is generated on the spot from retrieval injection by 32 and the model's latent knowledge.

15falsifiability filter — asks of every candidate obligation: "describe a concrete artifact that would make it fail." Ones that cannot be described (e.g. "the reasoning is coherent and elegant") are struck from the registry on the spot, leaving only a log. Rather delete than keep: the risk of deleting wrongly is backstopped by the coverage audit (holes get exposed by variants and loop three fills them back); keeping a hollow obligation that can never be verified deadlocks both ways — either someone ticks it by feel (tainting the certificate), or it stays unverified forever and jams the taint rule. The obligation list is a work order; an entry that cannot describe what failure looks like is not a check item, it is a sentiment.

16oracle enumerator — enumerates the checking methods available for the current obligation, ordering them into a ladder by descending soundness and de-correlation (cost-sensitive ordering is only a configurable option; the generation process is in §7). In the ladder for effect claims, the "audit instrument (25)" is the natural authority — the ledger is the source of truth for "happened" facts.

17variant generator — seeds from the candidate to generate "plausible-but-wrong variants". The same primitive serves 18's method testing and 22's coverage testing; its strength is continuously fed by 30's overturn records, and it is the adversarial upper bound of the whole system.

18discrimination tester — uses the current method to "verify" each variant: if any variant survives, the method is declared invalid and the verdict voided, forcing a switch to another method (on the main path, a downgrade); only rejecting all variants admits the method to binding. This is the first gate.

19evidence-contract binder — freezes a method that has passed both gates into an evidence-type contract: any verdict without evidence of that type is invalid. "The verification method is untrustworthy" thereby turns from a behavior problem into a contract problem.

20verifier executor — calls instruments per the contract to perform the discharge, producing evidence artifacts. Any crash, timeout, or unparseable output is recorded as a discharge failure (fail-closed), never converted into a pass.

21entailment auditor — checks item by item "does this evidence logically discharge this obligation": does the log belong to this script, does the proof contain sorry, can sampling evidence support a universal conclusion. This is the second gate; failures are sent back to 20 for redo.

22coverage auditor — attacks the entire obligation set with mutated answers: a variant passing everything = a hole in the obligation set, back to 14 for more obligations. Discovered holes and overturns are the main sources of 29 / 30.

23slice freezer — once the slice is all green, freezes the subgraph together with its evidence chain and exports it as the certificate. A pure function, no judgment.

Instruments & support (24–28)

24instrument provisioner — maintains three states for each instrument: ready / installable (installation cost counted into the ladder's est_cost; installed on first selection by the game, then cached permanently) / unavailable. "Not installed" is not a capability boundary but a one-time cost; unverified(no-instrument) is reserved for what genuinely cannot be installed (offline, policy-forbidden) or where no oracle exists at all. Installation happens inside the sidecar's own sandbox scope, fail-closed on failure.

25audit instrument — a special instrument with the event stream as its data source: it reconciles that "a call happened and returned X". The discrimination game for effect claims automatically lands on it (variants like "13 passed" or "collected 0" all die before the ledger) — tools therefore need no special treatment.

26instrument adapter — one instance per tool (python sandbox, Lean, retrieval, bash……), unified interface probe() / run(check) → artifact, run as subprocesses with timeouts and resource limits.

27termination controller — sets round caps for every loop (escalation/downgrade queue length, coverage-audit rounds, retry count), guaranteeing termination; cost is not this design's constraint — termination is. Reaching a cap downgrades to unverified(bounds), written honestly into the certificate; never a silent pass.

28cache — verification results keyed by (claim hash, obligation hash, instrument id, variant-set hash); when a claim is revised, the downstream dependency cone goes stale (a mechanical implementation of the blast radius). Content addressing makes the same claim verified only once across parallel branches.

Memory & products (29–34)

29obligation library — holes found by the coverage audit settle into obligation patterns (e.g. "financial figures must carry definitional obligations"), retrievable by 14 for reuse.

30variant pattern library — error patterns from overturned cases flow back, letting 17 generate harsher attacks.

31method policy library — records (claim features, method, whether overturned). Short-term it supports level-skipping; long-term it is ready-made reward data for training an instrument policy.

32memory retriever — injects the three libraries' contents into the templates of 14 / 16 / 17 in the guise of retrieval results. Templates stay generic forever — the landing point of "no domain knowledge in prompts".

33certificate — the frozen slice + each obligation's evidence and audit conclusions + graded verdict (proven / demonstrated / corroborated / unverified(reason)) + escalation traces and overturn history (holes are not hidden). It makes verification itself spot-checkable.

34feedback publisher — sends counterexamples and overturns back to the solver via MCP responses / hook injection. Arrival timing = the moment the solver starts investing in that claim, maximizing loss-cutting value; also the carrot that makes the solver willing to cooperate.

7 Mechanism A: the two gates — who adjudicates a method's efficacy and downgrade

The single most important mechanism in the system. The cost-agnostic strength-first policy eliminates the old question ("how strong a method should we use" — always go straight to the strongest reachable), but it buys two new questions that must be answered: is the strongest method really effective — might the formalization have proved something else (faithfulness), might the pipeline silently wave things through (fail-closed soundness)? When downgrade happens on unreachability, which level is enough — here "choosing strength" returns among the remaining methods. The variant exam's function is therefore forked: on the main path it examines validity — a false proposition must fail compilation under a sound prover, so if a variant somehow survives, what is exposed is a misconfigured pipeline (a smoke test), and the real information content lies in the variant exam of the faithfulness checker; on the downgrade path it resumes its original discrimination-selection function. The two gates adjudicate these two questions respectively.

Figure 4 · The two-gate loop body: the main path starts from the strongest reachable; exam failure or unreachability steps down; the referees are two kinds of recordable facts — variant life-or-death and evidence entailment

Main-path flow: go straight to the strongest reachable method; before accepting a verdict, pass two gates — the variant exam (gate one) and the evidence-entailment review (gate two). Any failure, or method unreachability (defined below), steps down one level and retries; the downgrade order and each level's gate outcomes are all recorded in the trace. Figure 4 draws this loop body; Figure 5 is the measured archive of the downgrade path: levels are descended only when formalization is unreachable, and the per-level gate records retain pedagogical value.

Figure 5 · Measured archive of the downgrade path: life-or-death records of variants at each level. Under strength-first the main path goes straight to formalization; this figure is only walked when downgrade-on-unreachability occurs

Three clarifications. First, L1–L5 in the figure are not a hard-coded ladder in the system, but a runtime product of the oracle enumerator (16) on this task: change the domain and the enumerated ladder is entirely different (statistics tasks get a "placebo test" level, fact-checking gets a "source-tracing retrieval" level). What the harness fixes is only an abstract vocabulary of evidence categories (text level / re-derive level / execution level / systematic-search level / formal level) — its identity is schema, not knowledge: it exists so certificates are comparable across tasks and the policy library has a stable feature space to learn from. Handing the ordering to the model is safe, because the stopping condition is the mechanical fact "all variants dead", not "reached some level" — ordering errors cost efficiency, never soundness. Second, "Lean exists" is not written in any prompt: it lives in the model's latent knowledge (the model proposes it itself during enumeration) and in the environment (the instrument provisioner ensures readiness: installed means usable, uninstalled means installed on first selection by the game). Third, once cost is not a constraint, the default policy is strength-first: go straight to the strongest applicable method on the ladder (formalization in the math domain); the two gates' role changes from "deciding how high to escalate" to "a validity exam for the strongest method"; only when the strongest method is unreachable (cannot formalize; library lookup and on-the-spot proof both fail) does the system step down to the next strongest, and a downgrade honestly caps the level. Figure 5's rung-by-rung climb therefore appears only on the downgrade path, as a measured record of unreachability.

Operational definition of "unreachable" (the mechanical trigger for downgrade): (i) instrument unavailable (no oracle of this kind / offline and installation forbidden); (ii) applicability failure (the proposition cannot be stated in that method); (iii) execution failures reaching the cap (timeouts, compilation failing N retries — judged by the termination controller and recorded in the trace). All three are mechanical events; downgrade never comes from judgment.

Division of labor between the two gates

Binding a method requires passing two independent gates in a row; both are indispensable. Gate one (discrimination test, 18) governs "can the method tell right from wrong": any surviving variant voids it. Gate two (entailment audit, 21) governs "is the evidence logically sufficient": even with all variants dead, if the submitted evidence does not entail the obligation, it is sent back all the same. The canonical case: for a universal obligation like "the proposition holds for all n", property testing (sampling for counterexamples) can kill every known variant — gate one passes — but its artifact is "no counterexample in a thousand samples", which cannot entail a universal conclusion, so gate two sends it back and escalation continues to formalization. Only when both gates nod does the contract hold.

How the ladder is generated (inside component 16)

Enumeration proceeds at obligation granularity. Four inputs, all runtime data: the current obligation, the instrument provisioner's (24) three-state list, policy-library (31) retrieval hits, and the fixed evidence-category vocabulary. Five steps:

obligation: {obligation}
claim features: {features}
instruments available in this environment (registry probe result): {registry}
history (retrieval hits): {memory_hits}

Enumerate all methods usable for checking this obligation. For each method output:
  procedure:          concrete checking steps
  instrument_id:      the instrument that runs it; must come from the instrument list above
  evidence_kind:      text | rederive | execute | search | formal
  artifact:           what evidence it will produce
  failure_looks_like: if the obligation fails, what the artifact would look like
  est_cost:           low | mid | high

① Method elicitation: one structured-output call, template as above, fully generic — "Lean can machine-check proofs" and "statistical conclusions admit placebo tests" flow out of the model's latent knowledge, triggered by the obligation and the instrument list. ② Mechanical grounding: each instrument_id is checked against the provisioner; methods referencing "installable" instruments are kept with installation cost counted in, and only the genuinely unavailable (offline / policy-forbidden / no oracle for this claim kind) are dropped. ③ Falsifiability filter: methods that cannot write failure_looks_like are dropped on the spot (a reuse of the same primitive as 15, which filters obligations). ④ Ordering: descending soundness (strength-first; cost-sensitive ordering is only a configurable option) → de-correlation; policy-library hits may jump straight to historical landing points. Ordering is only a heuristic and is not trusted. ⑤ Lazy consumption: the discrimination game pops from the queue head one at a time, compiles each into a check_spec, and fires variants at it; all variants dead plus gate two means binding, and queue exhaustion means an honest unverified(no-instrument). The three kinds of generation failure each have a backstop: wrong ordering costs only efficiency; bad methods die at the two gates; missed methods cost at worst an honest unverified. Enumeration results are cached keyed by (obligation hash + registry hash). Figure 5's L1–L5 are the projection of this process's output method list, merged by the vocabulary.

The spectrum of landing points: what lands on formalization under strength-first, what is forced to downgrade

The system never judges "how hard is this problem". Under strength-first, landing point = the strongest reachable method, and downgrade happens only on unreachability — each with its own enforcer: formalization reachable (statable, library lookup hit or on-the-spot proof succeeded, faithfulness exam passed) → proven; formalization unreachable but a complete execution check exists (finite universal, closed under substitute-back) → demonstrated; only review and blind re-proof available → capped at corroborated, and review must first pass the validity exam (measured discrimination on variants of that claim kind) and must produce a structured artifact with step-by-step reasons. Cold-start obligation completeness is under little pressure in the mechanically checkable domain: mathematical obligations are mostly derivable from logical form (quantifier structure, premise alignment, axiom list) and do not depend on domain conventions — convention-dependent definitional tasks belong to the extended domain (§13 example E marks this boundary explicitly).

This downgrade probing happens only the first time a claim kind is encountered: once the policy library records the landing point, similar claims get there in one step.

8 Mechanism B: three adversarial loops — one line of defense for each way verification can fail

For a wrong answer to sneak past verification, there are only three routes: missed checks (the obligation set is incomplete), shallow checks (the method too weak — pass does not entail correct), wrong checks (the evidence itself did not discharge the obligation). The system assigns one adversarial loop to each route; the three loops work at different levels, are mutually non-redundant, and are each anchored on a different criterion. Two honest notes: first, loop one and loop three share the variant generator (17) — error types it cannot create are blind spots of both loops, so the actual independence is "two and a half layers" rather than three, making the variant generator the most important single point in the system (its attack success rate is measured separately in evaluation); second, the mechanicalness of the criteria is graded — the proven level anchors on machine-checkable artifacts, while the entailment audit at the demonstrated / corroborated levels remains a compressed, focused LLM judgment: compressed, not eliminated.

Figure 6 · The three adversarial loops. A wrong answer must fool all three lines of defense at once; see the text for the mechanicalness grading of the defense criteria and the "two-and-a-half layers" correlation note

Each loop has its own dedicated memory: holes found by loop three settle into the obligation library (29), error patterns from overturned cases flow back into the variant pattern library (30), and loop one's final landing points are written into the method policy library (31). Every failure of the system has an explicit destination — this is the meta-harness's complete self-improvement circuit, and a ready-made data pipeline for future reinforcement learning.

9 Mechanism C: the single trigger rule — verification hangs on the dependency graph

"Verify only at the end / verify every step / verify on intermediate results / verify when tools are used" is not a policy to be chosen. The system has exactly one trigger rule; everything else is its corollary:

When an unverified claim gains its first dependent, verify it. (A certificate request = adding a dependent to final — a special case of the same rule.)
def on_edge_created(edge):                # called by the graph builder on every new edge
    if edge.tail.status == UNVERIFIED:
        enqueue(kernel.verify(edge.tail))     # async; the solver need not wait
def on_certificate_request(final):        # special case of the rule above
    for c in backward_slice(final):
        if c.status == UNVERIFIED:
            enqueue(kernel.verify(c))

This rule is the constructive version of the taint invariant (final must not depend on unverified): not a prohibition, but on-demand execution — at certification time the slice is naturally all green. Its byproduct properties: claims never depended upon are never verified; a refuted message arrives exactly when the solver starts investing in that claim, maximizing loss-cutting. Sensitivity is controlled by the extraction cadence knob (component 7): if extraction is hoarded until the end, the rule degenerates to "verify only at certification" (still sound, with all feedback crammed at the end); the more frequent the extraction, the earlier the triggers and the less rework — the knob affects only feedback latency, never correctness.

This rule is fired by five concrete events, all asynchronous except the third — the solver never stops working; the system has no timers and no polling: ① when the graph builder connects an edge (the most common; nobody "calls" it; sensitivity = extraction cadence); ② when the solver commits via MCP (declared edges take effect immediately — cooperation buys "earlier"); ③ the solver explicitly calls verify(target) (used by the protocol at key forks; the only synchronously awaited call); ④ Stop hook / certificate() / CI certification (= adding a dependent to final; the slice backstops); ⑤ search re-betting (adding try-effort to a branch = depending on its frontier claims — inspect the goods before betting).

Why tools need no special treatment

Under the premise "full trace retained, append-only", an effect claim ("tests ran and 12 passed") is just an ordinary assertion whose content is "this call happened", and its strongest yet cheapest verification method is the audit — reconciling the structured records in the event stream. Register "auditing" as an instrument (25), and the discrimination game automatically binds effect claims to it: variants ("13 passed", "collected 0", "a different command ran") all die before the ledger. The original design's "immediate post-condition check" has not disappeared — it has become the normal discharge of an effect claim upon first being depended on, going through exactly the same pipeline as every other claim. The cost, honestly recorded: traps like "an empty run still reported as success" are discovered at "first depended on" rather than "the moment of the call" — soundness intact, rework increased; and the audit can only prove "it returned 200 at the time", not "it is still healthy now" — the temporal semantics of effect claims are explicitly annotated by obligation compilation.

Why effect nodes are worth having

Three reasons. First, downstream claims really rest on it, yet the solver almost never says it out loud (it runs the tests and walks away) — nobody writes an assertion for "the algorithm really produced R", so the dependency chain would miss a link at certification; the parser ghost-writes it, and that is the effect node. Second, the ghost-writing happens at the moment of clearest information: a tool event is a structured record, and converting it to a node is deterministic code — zero cost, zero ambiguity; without the node, an LLM would have to fuzzily search the trace at certification time for "which call backs this assertion" — the same bookkeeping, done either for free at the clearest moment or for money at the fuzziest. Third, state anchoring: two "all tests green" events before and after a patch are two different facts about two versions of the world; effect nodes pin an assertion's semantics to "the world after which actions".

Figure 7 · How a bug-fixing trace becomes a claim graph: micro-steps folded, dead ends dropped, tool calls become effect nodes, conclusions become assertion nodes; F's dependency closure is the range that must be all green before certification

How the solver's self-checks are treated: reused, not trusted

Solvers often verify themselves (running tests, substituting back, reproducing). The verifier treats these self-checks under three identities; the criterion is the evidence contract, not "whether a check happened". First, tool-executed self-checks are evidence candidates: in the trace they are effect nodes, first-hand records — the run itself is reconciled by the audit instrument (it really ran, the output is as stated), and the run's semantics pass the entailment audit (does what the script checks line up with the obligation); passing both layers means direct adoption — the verifier does not rerun; qualified evidence already present is never remade. Second, verbal self-checks have zero evidentiary value: "I checked and it's fine" is just another assertion, no more credible than the original claim it checks. Third, contract gaps are filled by the verifier: if the method is too weak (sampling vs universal), escalate; if the obligation requires independence (blind re-solving), the solver's self-check is by definition disqualified and an independent role must redo it; even when reusing the solver's check script, that method still goes through the variant game (cheap: rerun the same script on the variants). In one sentence: the solver's self-checks are an evidence source awaiting audit, not a trusted conclusion source.

10 Mechanism D: one event stream — the thorough unification of online and offline

v0.1 unified the two modes with "two producers, one kernel"; v0.2 pushes the unification to the bottom: the system's only input is one event stream; "offline" is just a very-high-latency stream (replayed all at once), and "online" is just a very-low-latency stream (hooks pushing in real time). The graph builder and the kernel are latency-agnostic; even the trigger policy is no longer a component — triggers are themselves events in the stream. The sole duty of the adapters (1–4) is to normalize any source into the same kind of event.

The second layer of unification matters equally: the solver's cooperation is uniformly understood as "volunteer extraction". The claims it commits via MCP and the deps it declares are the same kind of thing as the output of our own extractors (7, 8) — proposals; the tamper auditor (9) treats all proposals alike. So the old problem "the solver's declarations are untrustworthy" needs no special handling: all proposers are untrusted, and all proposals pass the same audit. What cooperation buys is precision and saved extraction fees; soundness never passes through its hands — the overarching principle landing once again. The trigger profile thereby hugs the demand: the mechanical parts (effect nodes, audits) complete streaming-immediately; assertion extraction and kernel verification proceed on trigger — what laziness buys here is not savings but the tidy property that "the number of verification events is bounded by the number of load-bearing claims".

11 Mechanism E: verification-driven search

A trustworthy verifier can safely convert "more compute" into "higher correctness", and the conversion route is search. The bottleneck of best-of-N and beam search was never sampling — it is that the selection function is untrustworthy: LLM self-evaluation gets fooled by beautiful errors. This system's mechanical adjudication happens to be usable as a selection function, at claim granularity: pruning happens the moment a wrong assumption pokes its head out, not at the finish line.

Implementation adds no orchestration component at all: the solver explores in parallel with Claude Code's built-in subagents (the Task tool); orchestration is done by the solver itself, and we only supply a protocol prompt (CLAUDE.md). Subagent tool calls trigger hooks and enter the event stream all the same (payloads carry agent identifiers, and the graph builder tags branches accordingly), so the claim store naturally grows into an AND-OR graph: AND within a branch, OR between competing branches. Subagents also implement "private scratchpads" for free — independent contexts, discarded on exit.

# Verification cooperation protocol (CLAUDE.md fragment; verifier sidecar connected)

## Parallel exploration
- At key decisions with multiple possible paths (candidate root causes,
  candidate plans, proof routes, data definitions), launch 2–4 subagents
  in parallel with the Task tool, each exploring one mutually exclusive
  hypothesis; write the instruction as "establish or rule out this
  hypothesis", not "complete the whole task".

## Commit and adjudication
- After a subagent reaches a key conclusion, call
  verifier.commit(claim, deps) and request verify. Branch keep/drop
  decisions look only at the verdict:
  - refuted: abandon the hypothesis, record the counterexample in
    context, do not retry similar paths;
  - verified: keep going deeper; when multiple branches coexist, rank
    by evidence level.
- Do not eliminate branches by your own judgment; do not defend a
  hypothesis after a refuted.

## Wrap-up
- Before the final answer, call verifier.certificate(); handle any
  unverified / refuted on the slice first. Attach the certificate's
  conclusion level to the answer, relayed faithfully (including the
  overturn history).
Figure 8 · Parallel search with verification pruning: three mutually exclusive hypotheses set off simultaneously; refuted hard-kills, verified re-bets, and the certificate's slice automatically contains only the winner

Four search rules, all reading mechanical signals (note: "investment" in this section means try counts and exploration depth on the solver side; there is no cost budget inside the system, and the termination controller only enforces round caps): asymmetric pruning — branches are killed only on refuted (qualification: counterexample endorsement holds in the executable domain, which is this report's scope; in extended domains where obligations depend on domain conventions, refuted can also be wrong and should soften to strong down-weighting); verified levels are used only for ranking and re-betting (soft signals — wrong kills cost more than wrong keeps). Asset sharing — content addressing verifies the same claim only once across branches; refuted conclusions become global exclusions injected into sibling branches, and the search space shrinks monotonically (the shape of CDCL in SAT solvers: every rebuttal is a learned clause). Investment is use — adding try-effort to a branch is depending on its frontier claims, automatically triggering "inspect before betting" — still the same single rule. The protocol is advisory — the solver not parallelizing, not committing, or ignoring refuted does not affect soundness (the certificate gate does not move); it only wastes its own time; orchestration belongs to the generation side and affects only efficiency. Honest new problems: verification throughput becomes a shared bottleneck (the termination controller queues by branch state; verification demand from refuted branches dequeues directly, so the system automatically tilts toward "directions that verify"); same-base branches tend to think alike (diversity relies on the protocol explicitly assigning mutually exclusive hypotheses).

12 End-to-end main example: an Eulerian-circuit route (mechanically checkable domain)

User: "map.json is a pedestrian-street network; plan a patrol route that walks every street exactly once and returns to the start; the conclusion must be reliable." The solver's trace: read map.json and parse out graph G (tool → E1); run the connectivity check, True (→ E2); run the degree check, all even (→ E3); assert "connected and all degrees even, so by Euler's theorem a circuit exists" — the extractor splits this into A1 (connected), A2 (all degrees even), T (Euler's theorem: circuit ⟺ connected ∧ all degrees even, materialized as an ordinary node on the graph, sourced from solver memory), and A3 (a circuit exists); run Hierholzer to get route R (→ E4); conclusion F "follow R".

Figure 9 · The claim graph of the Eulerian-circuit task: theorem T is an ordinary node on the graph; orange circles are the trigger moments at which each node was verified

Moment 1 (A1, A2 enter the graph): E2, E3 are depended on for the first time → the audit instrument reconciles the event stream, verified in milliseconds. Moment 2 (A3 enters, three verifications in parallel): A1/A2 are cheap (the variant method confirms the check scripts' semantics); T goes strength-first straight to the formal level — mathlib lookup hits the corresponding theorem, and the remaining work is just three-way alignment (Lean statement ↔ natural-language theorem ↔ the direction A3 uses it), with the alignment check itself passing the variant exam (statements shifted into the "path version" or the "connectivity-dropped version" must all be caught) → proven, zero new proofs; had the lookup missed, it would formalize on the spot, and only further unreachability would step down to retrieval/review with an honest cap. Meanwhile O2 (premise alignment) is checked in the entailment audit: the theorem's premises {connected, all degrees even} ⊆ A3's verified dependencies {A1, A2} ✓. Counterfactual: the solver forgets to check connectivity (no A1) → T is still proven but O2 fails on the spot, A3 is rejected, and the feedback publisher sends back "missing connectivity premise" — citing a theorem while missing a premise is caught by the graph's structure, not by anyone's care. Moment 3 (E4 enters): only now is E1 depended on for the first time → audit. Moment 4 (F enters): A3's small-step entailment is checked in one step. Moment 5 (certification): F's obligation "R is valid" faces the coverage audit — mutant routes R′ (walking one street twice) and R″ (missing one street) break the obligation set that only says "the algorithm output R" → add the obligation "an independent checker verifies edge by edge: every street exactly once, end-to-end connected, back at the start" (a complete execution check independent of the Hierholzer implementation) → all mutants die. The coverage audit also launches an input-side attack: a mutant graph G′ (a version that silently drops one street during parsing) — G′ is connected ✓, all degrees even ✓, the circuit on it valid ✓, passing every existing obligation → exposing the hole at the very bottom of the chain: no obligation covers "G is faithful to map.json" (the audit only proves the parse call happened, not that parsing dropped no edges) → add the obligation "independent re-parse comparison: edge count and adjacency digest must match the original file" → G′ dies. This is the mirror image of the formalization gap on the input side (Lean statement ↔ original theorem; G ↔ map.json): every fact that enters the graph via parsing/loading automatically carries an input-faithfulness obligation — and this lesson was dug out by the coverage audit itself under cold start in the mechanically checkable domain, without relying on the memory libraries. Freeze and certify:

verdict: overall demonstrated (T alone proven; R's validity is a complete execution check)
slice: [F, A3, E4, E1, E2, E3, A1, A2, T]
T: { mathlib hit + three-way alignment, proven }    F: { coverage audit found two holes → independent edge-by-edge check + input-faithfulness comparison added }

Settling: the obligation library gains "graph-theoretic existence assertions must do premise alignment" and "parse-type effects must carry input-faithfulness obligations"; the variant library gains the "premise-deletion attack" and the "silent edge-drop attack"; the policy library records (theorem-citation kind → mathlib-lookup direct). The two key interceptions in this example — citing a theorem while missing a premise, and trusting only the algorithm's output — died to the graph structure and the coverage audit respectively; math-domain obligations (quantifiers, premises, axioms) are derived from logical form, with markedly weaker cold-start dependence than definitional-convention extended domains.

Example B · Fixing a bug (sidecar online throughout)

The solver fixes a 500 on a pagination endpoint. Hooks push every tool call into an effect node: the test baseline (E1, "12 passed" — the audit also reconciles "collected-case count matches baseline" alongside, guarding against the false pass of "an empty run still reported as success" — the classic scenario of a misconfigured case directory or a homegrown runner swallowing warnings); the reproduction (E2). The moment the root-cause assertion A1 gets depended on by the patch design, verification fires: the discrimination game finds the competing "off-by-one" hypothesis indistinguishable from A1 under ordinary reproduction → it further elicits a discriminating input pair (exactly-full page vs not-full page: A1 predicts (200,500), the competitor predicts (200,404)) → measured (200,500), the competitor dies. The endgame coverage audit builds a "symptom-only mutant patch" (catching the exception and swallowing it into an empty page) that breaks the obligation set containing only "reproduction turns green + suite all green" → add the obligation "direct unit test at the root-cause location + reproduction enters the regression suite" → mutant dies. verdict: demonstrated.

Example C · Checking a factual claim (extended domain · offline)

Verify ""Attention Is All You Need" was published at NeurIPS 2017". The model answers correctly from memory, but the discrimination test exposes: with the year variant changed to 2018, memory still nods with considerable confidence — memory's discrimination on details is unstable, verdict invalid, escalate to retrieving the primary source, evidence = proceedings snapshot + field-by-field comparison. The meaning of grading is most intuitive here: official source → demonstrated; only second-hand retellings → corroborated, requiring two independent sources; network unavailable → unverified(no-instrument), written honestly. The difference between "the model knows" and "it has been verified" is forcibly made explicit by the mechanism.

Example D · Verifying a statistical conclusion (extended domain · offline)

Verify "the A/B experiment shows a significant conversion-rate lift (p=0.03)". Obligations: definitions consistent with the experiment design (no peeking midway), test assumptions hold, conclusion robust to analytic degrees of freedom. The key level in method escalation: randomly shuffle the treatment labels and run the same analysis pipeline on the "placebo data" — a qualified procedure must come out "not significant"; still-significant means the procedure is manufacturing significance (a mechanical detection of p-hacking). This is exactly the negative control of scientific methodology: the discrimination test is not some strange thing we invented; it mechanizes the scientific community's scattered best practices into domain-agnostic components — and the placebo test was proposed by the model itself at runtime, corroborating that "no domain knowledge in prompts" is feasible.

Example E · Year-over-year sales monthly report (extended-domain demonstration: convention-dependent task)

For this kind of task, the system's true answer is "it knows after failing once" (the memory flywheel). The core promised scope (the mechanically checkable domain) relies on no such priors: mathematical obligations are mostly derived from logical form.

14 Data model and certificate

The system's only contract, fixed before any code.

Event {   # unified event stream: append-only JSONL; everything is an event
  ts, agent_id,                      # agent_id → branch label
  type: tool_call | tool_result | message | commit | stop | verify_request,
  payload
}
Claim {
  id:        content_hash            # content-addressed: dedup, caching, cross-branch reuse for free
  kind:      assertion | effect | final    # effect = mechanically ghost-written "happened" assertion
  text:      "..."
  deps:      [claim_id]              # proposals (extraction / commit), tamper-audited
  branch:    agent_id | null         # AND-OR: AND within a branch, OR between competitors
  revises:   claim_id | null         # revision chain: declared or extraction-inferred, tamper-audited; marks predecessor and its downstream stale
  status:    unverified | verified(grade) | refuted | stale
  obligations: [obligation_id]
}
Obligation { id, text, contract: { instrument_id, evidence_kind } }  # bound by 19
Evidence   { id: artifact_hash, instrument, artifacts: [...],
             discharges: obligation_id, entailment_audit: pass | fail }
Certificate {  # = frozen export of the backward slice from final (pure function)
  claim_graph_slice, obligations, evidence_chain,
  escalation_traces, coverage_audit_result, overturn_history,
  verdict: proven | demonstrated | corroborated | unverified(reason)
}

verdict level semantics: proven (machine-checkable formal evidence), demonstrated (execution-type evidence), corroborated (independent re-solves agree, discounted for same-base correlation), unverified(reason) (no method available / bounds exhausted, honestly annotated). The overall level = the weakest link on the slice, with itemized entries listed honestly. Weak evidence never collapses into a confident binary verdict.

15 Engineering checklist

Trusted computing base (TCB) statement

The certificate's trustworthiness ultimately rests on the correctness of the following deterministic components: event capture (the integrity of the hooks / log adapters), the append-only store of the event stream, the claim store state machine (including stale propagation and revision chains), the audit instrument, the cache-key logic, the slice freezer, and the Lean kernel. All LLM components (compilation, extraction, enumeration, variants, the two audits) are outside the TCB — their failures are backstopped by mechanisms. The threat model is limited to an "honest but error-prone" solver: a third-party agent's trace is self-reported, and the audit verifies ledger consistency, not reality; an adversarial solver would require signed event sources and trusted execution environments, which is out of scope.

  • fail-closed iron rule: instrument crashes, timeouts, unparseable outputs are uniformly recorded as discharge failures. No exceptional path may be converted into a pass.
  • roles are stateless sub-calls: each LLM role = a generic prompt template + a JSON schema, with error-bearing retries on validation failure; zero shared context between roles (the structural guarantee of audit independence).
  • cache key: (claim_hash, obligation_hash, instrument_id, mutant_set_hash), all four content hashes; claim revision → downstream dependency cone goes stale; automatic dedup across parallel branches.
  • hook payloads carry agent identifiers: branch labels for free; PostToolUse is the effect-event source; PreToolUse is used only for the optional outbound-action gate.
  • extraction cadence is the only latency knob: it affects only how early feedback arrives, never correctness; cooperative commits make triggers earlier and more accurate.
  • asymmetric pruning: branches are killed only on refuted (hard evidence); verified levels are used only for ranking and re-betting — wrong kills cost more than wrong keeps.
  • termination caps: cost is not the design constraint — termination is; every loop carries a round cap, and hitting the cap downgrades to unverified(bounds), written into the certificate; audits are never silently cut.
  • independence discount: "independent re-solves" by same-base agents are discounted; oracle ordering adds a de-correlation dimension beyond soundness (different modalities, different tool paths preferred).
  • variant strength is the upper bound: a mutator that only generates dumb errors creates a false "no need to escalate". Mitigation: overturn patterns flow back continuously (30), and the variant library's attack success rate is measured separately in evaluation.
  • formalization's soft joint: what Lean can prove is its statement, not the original theorem; "statement faithfulness" must be an explicit obligation, and the faithfulness checker itself passes the variant method.
  • tech stack: Python + SQLite + JSONL; role calls go through the Claude API structured output; instruments are all subprocess adapters; attachment = two lines of Claude Code hooks config + MCP registration.

16 Build order

Figure 11 · Five milestones, each with a measurable acceptance criterion; M2's and M3's criteria are themselves tests of architectural unification and search value

M0 uses the file replay adapter to get the whole chain working end to end first (no integration at all, thinnest slice); M1 is the viability experiment: if the discrimination game cannot significantly lower the false-pass rate, nothing after it is worth doing; M2 adds only adapters and graph construction — the kernel must change zero lines; M3 adds only protocol text and branch-label handling; M4 makes the system grow stronger with use.

17 Evaluation plan

  • primary metric: false-pass rate — the proportion that passes verification but is labeled wrong; the direct measure of failure root two; must improve significantly over the LLM-as-judge baseline.
  • search gain: at a fixed try count, the pass rate of "subagent parallelism + verification pruning" against two baselines: "best-of-N without verification" and "single thread".
  • wrong-kill rate: the proportion of correct branches killed by refuted pruning (should be near zero, counterexample-endorsed).
  • secondary metrics: false-reject rate; overturn latency; variant-library attack success rate (measuring 17's strength upper bound); extraction cadence vs rework curve.
  • ablation: all mechanisms are config switches (no variants / no escalation / no coverage audit / no memory / no protocol); the event stream natively supports replay-based ablation.

18 Limitations and open problems

  • The infinite regress can only be compressed, never eliminated. The entailment auditor is an LLM too. The defense is structural: each layer up, the task is smaller and more mechanical; the bottom anchors on machine-checkable artifacts; and the certificate makes spot-rechecking extremely cheap.
  • The variant generator is the strength upper bound of the whole system. Error types it cannot generate are invisible to both the game and the coverage audit; the backflow mechanism strengthens it with failures, but cold start needs calibration with a known-wrong answer set.
  • The latency cost of extraction cadence. Effect traps (like an empty run still reporting success) are discovered at first-dependence rather than at call time; soundness intact, rework increased.
  • The temporal semantics of effect claims. The audit proves "it returned X at the time", not "it is still so now"; environment drift must be modeled explicitly at the obligation layer.
  • The protocol is advisory. A solver ignoring the protocol loses only efficiency, but heavy non-cooperation squeezes all verification to certification time — the feedback's loss-cutting value drains away with it.
  • Same-base correlation. Both independent re-solving and branch diversity are discounted; de-correlation ordering and explicit mutually exclusive hypotheses mitigate but do not eliminate; mixing multiple base models is the natural next step.
  • Unformalizable claims. Subjective argumentation has no strong oracle to escalate to; the correct behavior is to output unverified with an honest explanation — "knowing you cannot verify" is itself a valuable output.

19 Implementation and experiments (v0.4 addition)

This chapter reports how the design landed: every mechanism of §1–§18 is implemented as a runnable system (Python, ~2,600 lines of core + 125 offline tests + 5 live acceptance tests) and has passed the viability experiments planned in §17. More importantly: the experiments forced three design-level gaps into the open — exactly the methodology this report repeatedly advocates, applied to itself: do not trust "looks right"; make failures mechanically observable; then fix what was observed.

19.1 Implementation map

19.2 Viability experiments

Benchmark: 12 mechanically checkable mathematical propositions (6 true + 6 subtly false — e.g. "for all 1≤n≤1000, 12 divides n³−n", which holds at n=1 and is exposed at n=2). Both arms use the same model (claude-sonnet-5): the direct-judge arm = one-shot judgment without tools; the verifier arm = the full kernel. The key metric is false-pass (verifying a false one as true — the only fatal category); verifier unverified counts as abstention, never converted into a pass.

Two readings: first, this benchmark is too easy for sonnet-5 direct judging (12/12), so the discrimination lies not in accuracy but in every verdict carrying an independently recheckable evidence chain (check scripts, run outputs, audit records, frozen by content addressing), and in abstaining rather than guessing when verification cannot move — run1's 6 abstentions all landed on true propositions, and not one false proposition slipped through: fail-closed directionality never broke. Second, the run1→run2 gap comes entirely from the three mechanism fixes below, not from prompt tuning.

19.3 Three design corrections forced out by the experiments

Fix one: exam questions must be pattern-ported. Obligations are functions of the claim, not constants. Compiled from "3 | n⁵−n for all n", an obligation is an arithmetic fact that holds for any variant claim — taking its literal text to examine a "60 divides" variant, the correct method must pass it, and then gets wrongly convicted of a "false pass". The discrimination exam now ports the obligation as a pattern onto the variant and checks isomorphic instances (mechanism text, zero domain knowledge). This fills a premise that was implicit and unstated in §7's two-gate narrative.

Fix two: the examiner itself must also be examined. When a variant is passed by some method, there are two suspects: the method (a blind spot) or the exam question itself (the generator misjudged its falsity, or it is false in a direction this obligation does not govern). Now an independent agent first re-runs the disproof to quality-check the question: only on confirming "not actually false / off-target" is the question struck (event variant_disqualified), with the striking reason fed back to the generator for a regeneration limited to one round; if the quality check is inconclusive, the question stays and the method is rejected — the failure direction is always fail-closed, and an empty exam admits no method. This is loop two (wrong checks → send back) applied to the exam itself, giving §8's "two-and-a-half layers" correlation risk a mechanical line of defense.

Fix three: obligations must be world-level conditions. The correct corollary of answer-blindness is stronger than what v0.3 wrote: the compiler cannot see the argument, so argument-facing obligations like "the proof must provide a certain identity transformation / cover a certain case" are a category error — the argument's intermediate steps in this design enter the graph as independent claims, each verified on its own (§9); they neither need nor permit management by meta-obligations. Before the contract was tightened, the compiler produced 7 obligations for one divisibility proposition — many of them argument-facing, none verifiable — and every true proposition abstained; after tightening, they collapsed to 1–2 apt truth conditions, all abstentions converted to verified, and most bound formal instruments at proven.

19.4 Engineering reality: the price of strength-first and the countermeasures

Strength-first cashed out more thoroughly than expected in real runs: even for a proposition like "1 to 100 sums to 5050", the oracle enumerator went straight to Lean — and one discharge agent pulled the entire mathlib (7.3 GB) into the working directory on the spot, the artifact snapshot swallowed it whole into the content-addressed store (1.1 GB), and the disk filled up. The countermeasures are all mechanized: snapshots exclude toolchain directories (.git/.lake/node_modules…) with file-count and total-size caps, truncation leaving a trail; heavyweight dependencies install into a toolchain directory shared across checks (the landed form of component 24's "installation is a one-time cost"); the working directory is cleaned after snapshotting (the snapshot is the evidence; the directory is scratch). Another lesson: the CLI reports 429 rate-limiting as the opaque "error result: success" — the SDK backend now classifies by api_error_status and retries with exponential backoff, so transient failures no longer masquerade as generation failures. Component 27 (termination controller) landed as an obligation-count cap: over the cap means no verification and an honest unverified(bounds) — better to admit not verifying than to verify a subset and pass it off as the whole.

Not yet done, honestly listed: mathlib cache warm-up for the Lean full-formalization path (currently pulled fresh every time — slow but correct); harder benchmarks (problems where direct judging errs, so the two arms' accuracies can pull apart); a live end-to-end demo of the hooks attachment points (scripts and ingestion are tested, but have not been run against a real Claude Code session); cross-session effectiveness experiments for the three memory libraries (wired up, entering only prompt slots, benefits not yet quantified).

This appendix is the reference script for implementing M0/M1: for one concrete claim, it lists each component's actual inputs and outputs step by step. The verified claim C (submitted by the solver, with a proof): "for every positive integer n, 6 divides n³−n. Proof: n³−n = (n−1)·n·(n+1); among three consecutive integers one must be divisible by 2 and one by 3, so the product is divisible by 6." Environment: python+sympy, lean4+mathlib, and retrieval all ready; policy library cold-started; policy is strength-first (cost is not a constraint); the downgrade path is also recorded to demonstrate the two gates.

Steps 10–11 · dual audits (21, 22): the entailment audit's mechanical items (hash ↔ file, exit 0, grep finds no sorry, axioms ⊆ the standard three) + the LLM small-step item (statement faithfulness: Lean states all of ℤ ⊇ ℤ⁺, which is stronger, so entailment holds and is recorded in the comparison; the faithfulness checker itself passes the variant exam — the 4∣ version, the n³+n version, and the ZMod 5 version must all be caught, otherwise the checker is voided and rerun). The coverage audit's forgery attack: an artifact that compiles but proves a different proposition → rejected by the faithfulness obligation ✓; a sorry version → rejected by grep ✓. No holes.

Contrast (easy case): if C were "the solutions of x²−5x+6=0 are 2 and 3", strength-first likewise goes straight to formalization (factorization + mul_eq_zero, a few lines; illustrative) → proven; the execution-level complete substitute-back check is the downgrade fallback when formalization is unreachable. Not one step of the whole journey is "judging how hard it is": every advance is the same loop body — take the strongest available method → validity exam (gate one) → review evidence type (gate two) → bind or downgrade.
